The rent is set in a spreadsheet nobody elected

Four software firms now price a third of new leases in Melbourne. We read 2,100 of their quotes and found the same rounding rule in every one.

By Hana Whitlock, with data by Rui Tanaka / 18 October 2026 / 22 min read

Fact checked Save for later

In March, a two-bedroom flat in Brunswick East was listed at $685 a week. The landlord, a retired pharmacist who owns three properties, told us she had wanted $660. The number came from a dashboard her agent subscribes to, which recommended a figure and then rounded it up to the nearest five.1

That rounding rule, small on any single lease, appears in every quote we collected. Across 2,100 listings from January to June, 61 percent of weekly rents ended in five and 33 percent ended in zero. Rents set by people alone, in a sample from 2017, ended in five only 22 percent of the time.

Four firms supply the software. None of them sets a rent directly, and all of them say the landlord has the last word. In practice, agents told us, the recommended figure is the figure. One said that departing from it “means writing an email to explain yourself to head office.”

The extra five dollars is not the story. Multiplied across 38,000 new leases a year, it moves about $9.9 million from tenants to owners annually in one city, before any change in the market itself.2 The larger question is who checks the setting.

Victoria's rental code covers how often rent can rise and how much notice a tenant must get. It says nothing about how the first number is chosen. The state's consumer office told us it had “no current inquiry” into pricing software and did not say whether one was planned.

The firms are not hiding the rule. Two of them describe it in their documentation as “psychological pricing alignment.” What none of them publishes is how often the recommended rent is accepted unchanged. One agent's records, which we reviewed, put it at 94 percent.3

Method

We collected public listings from three portals between 4 January and 28 June 2026 and matched each to its pricing supplier through agent disclosures.
